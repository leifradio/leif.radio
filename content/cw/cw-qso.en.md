A typical CW QSO often follows the same pattern, a so-called *rubber stamp* QSO. You exchange a signal report, name, QTH and perhaps a little about equipment and the weather, and then thank each other for the contact. The pattern is a good place to start because you know what comes next. That makes it much easier to keep up with the pace.

Here is how a QSO between me and a German station might look:

{{< qso me="SM4T" other="DL1ABC" >}}
{{< qso-tx call="SM4T" >}}
CQ CQ CQ DE SM4T SM4T K
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="DL1ABC" >}}
SM4T DE DL1ABC DL1ABC K
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="SM4T" >}}
DL1ABC DE SM4T GM DR OM TNX FER CALL
UR RST 579 579
NAME LEIF LEIF
QTH BORLANGE BORLANGE
HW? AR DL1ABC DE SM4T K
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="DL1ABC" >}}
SM4T DE DL1ABC R GM LEIF TNX FB RPT
UR RST 559 559
NAME HANS HANS
QTH MUNICH MUNICH
HW? AR SM4T DE DL1ABC K
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="SM4T" >}}
DL1ABC DE SM4T R TNX HANS
RIG FLEX 8400 ES ANT VERTICAL
WX CLOUDY TEMP -3C AR
DL1ABC DE SM4T K
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="DL1ABC" >}}
SM4T DE DL1ABC R TNX INFO LEIF
RIG IC7300 100W ES ANT DIPOLE
WX SUNNY 8C
TNX FER QSO ES 73 AR SM4T DE DL1ABC SK
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< qso-tx call="SM4T" >}}
DL1ABC DE SM4T TNX FER FB QSO HANS
QSL VIA LOTW
73 ES GL AR DL1ABC DE SM4T SK TU EE
{{< /qso-tx >}}
{{< /qso >}}

**Common abbreviations**

- `CQ` – general call
- `DE` – from
- `K` – go ahead (over)
- `AR` – end of message
- `SK` – end of contact
- `R` – received
- `TNX` – thanks
- `UR` – your
- `RST` – signal report (readability, strength, tone)
- `QTH` – location
- `HW?` – how did you copy?
- `FB` – fine
- `RIG` – radio
- `ANT` – antenna
- `WX` – weather
- `ES` – and
- `73` – best regards
- `TU EE` – "thank you" and a double-click as a sign-off

**Tip:** Repeat name and QTH twice so the other station does not have to ask again. Keep the pattern in front of you the first few times. Soon it will be second nature, and you can put more energy into listening.
